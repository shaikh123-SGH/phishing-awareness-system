Why it matters

Most online fraud succeeds because of one missed warning sign

Scams rarely rely on advanced hacking. They rely on urgency, trust and a moment of distraction. A message that threatens account closure, an offer that expires in minutes, a caller who sounds official — each is designed to stop you from checking. Awareness closes that gap.

{{ ico.icon('alert') }}

Pressure is the weapon

Fraud messages create panic or excitement so you act before verifying. Recognising that pressure is itself the defence.

{{ ico.icon('users') }}

Anyone can be targeted

Students, homemakers, shopkeepers and professionals are all approached. Losses come from missing a signal, not from a lack of intelligence.

{{ ico.icon('check') }}

Prevention is learnable

A short list of habits — verify the sender, never share codes, check the address — prevents the majority of everyday scams.
