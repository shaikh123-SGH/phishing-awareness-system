I think I got scammed

Take a breath. The first hour matters more than anything else. Work through these steps in order.

  1. Stop all further communication and payment

    Do not send another rupee, do not share any more information, and do not continue the conversation — not even to argue or to try to recover the money. Continuing is exactly what a second-stage scam depends on.

  2. Contact your bank or payment provider immediately

    If money is involved, call the number printed on your card, your passbook or the official app. Ask them to block the card, freeze the account or raise a dispute on the transaction. Note the complaint reference they give you.

  3. Secure the affected accounts

    From a different, clean device, change the passwords of the affected accounts and your email. Sign out of all active sessions, enable two-factor authentication, and remove any app the fraudster asked you to install.

  4. Preserve the evidence

    Take screenshots of the messages, chat threads, caller number, web address, transaction IDs, amounts and timestamps before anything is deleted. Keep them together in one folder; you will need them for both the bank dispute and the official complaint.

  5. Report through official cybercrime channels

    File a complaint with the official cybercrime reporting channel and, where applicable, the local police, using the evidence you collected. Report as early as possible — the speed of reporting has a direct effect on whether a transaction can be held or reversed.

Also worth doing

  • Tell someone you trust. Embarrassment is the main reason scams go unreported.
  • Warn your contacts if your account may have been used to message them.
  • Check your statements for the next few weeks for further unauthorised activity.
  • Review which apps and permissions exist on your phone and remove anything unfamiliar.
