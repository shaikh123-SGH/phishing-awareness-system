Semester 5 · Community Engagement Project

About this project

A community-facing cybersecurity awareness platform that teaches people to recognise phishing, online fraud and payment scams — and to respond correctly when targeted.

Introduction

Project introduction

Digital payments, online shopping and messaging apps are now part of daily life for students, families and small businesses. The same convenience has given fraudsters a low-cost route to a very large audience. This project builds an interactive awareness platform that explains how common scams work, lets people practise identifying them on realistic fictional examples, and measures whether awareness actually improved.

Problem statement

The problem being addressed

People fall victim to online scams not because the attacks are technically advanced, but because the warning signs are unfamiliar. Awareness material that does exist is often scattered, text-heavy, written in technical language, or purely one-way. There is little opportunity to practise recognition, and almost no way to check whether a session actually changed behaviour.

Need

Why the project is needed

  • Scam techniques change faster than printed awareness material can be revised.
  • Community members need plain-language explanations, not technical jargon.
  • Recognition is a skill, and skills need practice rather than reading alone.
  • Victims often do not know the correct first steps, which delays reporting.
  • Awareness programmes rarely measure whether understanding improved.
Aim

Aim of the project

To improve community awareness of phishing, online fraud and payment scams through an accessible, interactive platform, so that participants can identify suspicious communication, prevent avoidable losses, and respond safely and quickly if targeted.

Objectives

  • Explain twelve common scam categories in simple, structured language.
  • Provide an interactive Spot the Scam exercise using fictional examples.
  • Measure awareness through a pre-test and a post-test with comparison.
  • Offer curated videos, checklists and printable material for field sessions.
  • Give clear emergency guidance for people who have already been targeted.
  • Collect feedback so the material can be improved after each session.

Social impact

Expected social impact

Every person who learns to pause before clicking protects not only their own savings but also the contacts a compromised account would have been used to target. The platform is designed to be used in classroom sessions, neighbourhood workshops and self-paced study, with the same content available afterwards for revision and for sharing at home.

  • Fewer avoidable financial losses in the participating community.
  • Faster and more confident reporting when an incident does happen.
  • Awareness that spreads outward from each participant to their family.
Scope

Project scope

In scope: awareness education, interactive recognition practice, measurement of learning through pre-test and post-test, curated videos, downloadable material, emergency guidance and administrator-managed content.

Out of scope: investigating incidents, recovering money, scanning links or files, legal advice, and any handling of real financial credentials.

Privacy and safety notice: the platform stores only a name, email, optional participation details and quiz results. It never asks for and must never be given a real password, OTP, PIN, card number or banking credential. All scam examples are fictional.
